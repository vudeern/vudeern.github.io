My role

As co-founder and Head of Research, I decide which questions we pursue through NEXUS and how those findings should inform our architecture work. I also serve as the lab’s connection to UT Austin.

What we’re studying

Our starting question is about selection: when information arrives, how does a brain decide what needs to become active? We’re studying that behavior for principles that could inform computing systems.

The ambition is to carry those principles into both AI architecture and chip design. Selective computation and energy use guide the work; the research is still underway.

Research at the lab

NEXUS

NEXUS is where we test the questions against public brain data. We define what would count as a passing result before running an experiment, then use the measurements to guide the next step.

Code is being released as the work develops, and papers are in preparation.

NEXUS on GitHub ↗

Bio-intelligent architecture

The next question is architectural: can a system use its own state to decide which parts should engage? This work is intended to translate findings from NEXUS into a different way of organizing AI.

Hardware co-design

The architecture also has to inform what it runs on. Our longer-term direction is to develop models and neuromorphic hardware together, with research and hardware engineering feeding into each other.
